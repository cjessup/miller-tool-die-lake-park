# Miller Tool & Die — research notes

The live site at https://www.miller-tool.com/ is a small static shop site (Adobe GoLive 4 / FrontPage) for **Miller Tool & Die**, a full-service design and fabrication facility in Lake Park, Florida. The homepage states the line of business directly: tools, dies, jigs, fixtures, gages, prototypes, and specialty machining, from medical fixturing to aerospace support tooling and ground handling equipment. Machine work listed on the site includes CNC milling, EDM, turning, heat treat, welding, and quality control. Design tools named on the site are Unigraphics, Autocad, and Pro Mechanica.

## Original palette and voice

- Homepage is white with black Arial text and a bright-blue script wordmark, `title.jpg` ("Miller Tool & Die"), with a soft gray shadow.
- The Information page uses solid bars at `#3399ff`.
- Inner pages use separate pastel table backgrounds (peach design, mint engineering, cyan fabrication, khaki quality, lavender capabilities, silver gallery). Those tints were page fills, not a single brand system.
- Gallery heading was set in Elephant. Body copy is plain Arial.
- The shop photo shows a cream industrial building with turquoise roll-up doors. That turquoise stays in the photograph. The rebuild uses the wordmark blue, the `#3399ff` bar, white, and steel gray so the interface is not a generic teal theme.
- Mood: a working Florida toolroom — CAD and finite-element pictures, machined fixtures, a CMM, and an American flag on the homepage. Practical, not decorative.

## Peer sites

Layout ideas only. No text, photos, or branding were copied.

### 1. RAM Tool, Inc. — https://www.ramtoolinc.com/

Precision die-cast dies, EDM, and CNC machining in Grafton, Wisconsin.

Borrowed:

- A full-width shop photograph first, with the company name and a quotation action directly under it (no text sitting on the photo).
- A numbered capability rail (their 01–05 services) used here for Miller’s own pages: Design, Engineering, Fabrication, Quality, and the rest of the published nav.
- An industry row limited to industries the shop already names.
- A work-gallery band of real parts before the closing quotation ask.

### 2. Mahuta Tool Corporation — https://www.mahutatool.com/

CNC machining and tool-and-die work (dies, jigs, fixtures, gages, EDM) in Germantown, Wisconsin.

Borrowed:

- Two clear capability groups on the homepage — design/engineering on one side, shop processes on the other — as compact lists, not long essays.
- The phone number treated as a primary action, including in a persistent mobile call bar.
- EDM and machining called out beside a real shop photograph, with the detailed machine list on its own page.
- Short service chips instead of large padded cards.

### 3. Verdugo Tool and Engineering — https://www.verdugotool.com/

Tool and die design, tooling, and metalwork in Los Angeles.

Borrowed:

- A tight row of service links immediately under the hero so a buyer can jump to Design, Engineering, Fabrication, or Quality without scrolling the whole site.
- The “designers and builders together” story (already Miller’s “total package” line) placed as paired photo-and-sentence rows.
- Industry names as small chips, not illustrated marketing tiles.
- Contact repeated after the work, with the published phone and email, rather than only in a header.
